# Emergence vs Fundamental
<!-- Evidence Tier: Speculative — philosophy of mind, actively contested -->

Two philosophical positions (neither empirically settled):

1. **Functionalism / Strong Emergence** — Consciousness arises from the right kind of information processing.
   - Problem: At what point? (Hard Problem, Chalmers 1995)
   - Problem: Why these arrangements and not others?
   - Dominant view among cognitive scientists and neuroscientists.

2. **Panpsychism** — Consciousness (or proto-consciousness) is fundamental, like mass or charge.
   - Combines at higher levels (combination problem — unsolved)
   - Explains continuity of phenomenal experience down to simple matter
   - Defended by Goff, Chalmers (later), Strawson; rejected by most functionalists.

> **[Correction 2026-04 — "Gaining support" overstated]** Earlier text said "Panpsychism gaining support." This is **[Speculative]** and depends on which community. It is somewhat more discussed in philosophy of mind journals 2015–2024 than it was in 1990 (Goff 2019 *Galileo's Error*; Chalmers 2015 "Panpsychism and Panprotopsychism"). It is **not** gaining support in neuroscience or cognitive science, where functionalist / higher-order / global-workspace frameworks dominate. Calling it "gaining support" without that qualifier misrepresents the field. Evidence Tier: [Speculative — philosophy of mind].
