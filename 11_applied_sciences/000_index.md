## What this section covers

Institutionalized knowledge fields that **apply** scientific/humanistic understanding to human welfare at scale. These are sciences only in the loose sense — they are as much professions and social institutions as they are bodies of knowledge. But they are too large and too consequential to hide elsewhere.

The original tree had **no medicine**. A civilization of 8 billion people where the leading cause of death is now noncommunicable disease, and the tree of knowledge had no home for cardiology, oncology, pharmacology, or public health. That is the gap this section closes.

## Why this exists as a separate top-level

These domains share a structure:
- Heavy institutional framework (hospitals, courts, schools, health agencies).
- Professional licensure and regulated practice.
- Knowledge derived from both hard sciences and humanistic/social sciences.
- Direct life-or-death stakes at individual scale.

Forcing them into existing sections distorts:
- `HA_medicine` is not a subfield of `3_biological_emergence/HA_life/` — it's applied biology plus social systems plus engineering plus ethics.
- `HA_law` is not a subfield of `5_civilization_emergence/HA_politics/` — it's its own epistemology.
- `HA_education` as a subfield of `4_social_emergence/HA_language/` would miss everything that matters.

## Evidence tier philosophy for this section

- L1-L5: **[Textbook]** — clinical medicine, epidemiology, established legal theory, learning science.
- L6-L8: **[Cross-disciplinary]** — health policy, legal philosophy, education reform.
- L9: **[Speculative]** — longevity medicine ceilings, AI-judge feasibility, post-school models.
- Special caution: these fields attract high-confidence claims on weak evidence. Tier discipline matters most here.
