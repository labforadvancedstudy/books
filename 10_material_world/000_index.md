## What this section covers

How humans turn matter and energy into usable things at civilizational scale. This is the **supply side** of civilization — what the existing tree treated as an afterthought hidden inside `HA_technology/` or not at all.

Distinct from `6_technological_emergence` (information technology, computation, AI) and `13_engineering` (design disciplines). This section covers the **stuff flow**: atoms → materials → goods → calories → energy → logistics.

The original tree jumped from "society" to "computers" and largely skipped the two hundred years of industrial civilization that produced today's material base. Creating this section makes the gap explicit.

## Why this exists as a separate top-level

- `6_technological_emergence` is currently information-centric and has no natural home for steelmaking, fertilizer, or container shipping.
- These are not "subfields of technology" — they each run civilizations and have their own L0-L9 depth.
- Hiding them inside `HA_technology/` understates their weight. The Haber-Bosch process alone feeds ~4 billion people; it deserves more than a paragraph.

## Evidence tier philosophy for this section

- L1-L6: **[Textbook]** — materials science, process engineering, agronomy, grid engineering are mature.
- L7-L8: **[Cross-disciplinary]** — industrial policy, supply chain economics, food geopolitics.
- L9: **[Speculative]** — room-temp superconductors, self-replicating factories, post-scarcity claims.
