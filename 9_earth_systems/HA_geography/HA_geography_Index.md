## What this book covers

Geography as the study of spatial distribution: physical geography (landforms, biomes), human geography (population, settlement, culture), biogeography (species ranges), political geography (borders and their consequences), and the methods (maps, GIS, remote sensing) that make all of it legible.

## Why this book exists

Geography is often taught as "memorize capital cities," which misses everything important. Real geography is the discipline of *why things are where they are* — and it quietly determines economic development, military conflict, resource access, and cultural contact. Jared Diamond-style geographic determinism is overstated but the underlying observation (place matters) is solid.
